Glossary

Plain-language explanations of the terms this app (and roasting in general) throws around — both the coffee-roasting vocabulary (BT, Turning Point, First Crack...) and the hardware/Modbus terms that show up on the Direct Modbus connection screens (Slave ID, Register, Divisor...). Written for someone who isn't an engineer or a professional roaster — if a definition still assumes knowledge you don't have, that's a bug in the definition, not you. Terms in bold within a definition link to their own entry.

Terms tagged Modbus / FZ-94 are specific to how this app talks to real roaster hardware over Modbus — the example numbers given are for the one machine this app ships detailed support for, the Coffee-Tech FZ-94 (see the FZ-94 page for the full citation trail). A different Modbus roaster would have its own, different numbers for the same concepts.

A B C D E F H K M P R S T V W

A

Air
One of the three things this app can control on a real roaster (along with Burner and Drum) — the fan that pushes air through the roast chamber, carrying heat and smoke/chaff out. More airflow generally cools/slows the roast and helps clear smoke; less airflow holds heat in. The Air slider always shows what the fan is actually doing right now, read back from the hardware itself, not just a memory of the last command sent.

B

Baud rate Modbus / FZ-94
How fast data moves over the wire between this app and the roaster, in bits per second. Both ends have to be set to the exact same number or the connection simply won't work — it's not something you tune for better performance, just a setting that has to match. The FZ-94 uses 19200.
BT (Bean Temperature)
How hot the actual coffee beans are right now. This is the number most roasters watch closest — it climbs, mostly steadily, from room temperature up past 200°C over the course of a roast, and every milestone (Turning Point, Dry End, First Crack...) is defined by what BT is doing.
Burner
One of the three things this app can control on a real roaster — the heat source. On most roasters this is a straightforward power percentage; on the FZ-94 specifically it's stranger — see SV.
Bytesize Modbus / FZ-94
How many bits make up one character sent over the serial connection. Almost always 8 (the FZ-94 included) — part of the same "both ends must agree exactly" framing as Baud rate, Parity, and Stop bits.

C

CHARGE
The moment green (unroasted) coffee beans go into the hot roaster. This is when the clock starts — every other milestone's timing is measured from Charge, at 0:00.
Control channel Device Profile
One of the three adjustable things a Device Profile knows how to operate on a given roaster — Burner, Air, or Drum — along with exactly which register(s) and mechanism it takes to move that one.
COOL END
The moment the post-roast cooling cycle finishes and the beans have dropped down to a safe handling temperature.

D

Damper
A physical flap some roasters have that controls how much air/smoke flows through the machine, independent of the fan itself. Shown on the chart as an option if your setup reports one — most direct-Modbus setups (including the FZ-94) don't have this wired up as a separate control.
Device Profile
A named, saved "map" this app uses to talk to one specific roaster brand/model over Modbus — which temperature probes exist and where to find them, which controls exist and how to move them. Building one once (or picking the built-in FZ-94 profile) means this app can support a new roaster without anyone writing new code.
DEV% (Development percent)
What percentage of the whole roast, so far, was spent in the development phase — the stretch from First Crack Start to Drop.
DEV TIME (Development time)
How long the development phase (First Crack Start to Drop) has lasted, or is currently lasting, in minutes:seconds.
Direct register Modbus
The simplest of the three ways this app can write a control value over Modbus — one plain number written straight to one register, no separate "start" command needed first. Plenty of non-FZ-94 roasters use this simpler mechanism for a fan or damper.
Divisor Modbus / FZ-94
A roaster's controller often stores a temperature as a plain whole number scaled up, rather than as a decimal — cheaper and simpler for the hardware to handle. E.g. 96.5°C gets stored as the whole number 965. The divisor (10, on every FZ-94 probe) is what undoes that scaling back into a real, readable temperature: 965 ÷ 10 = 96.5.
DROP
The moment the beans are removed from the roaster (dumped into the cooling tray). Marks the end of active roasting/development and the start of the cooling phase, right before Cool End.
DRY END
The moment the beans finish driving off their internal moisture and the roast moves into the browning ("Maillard") stage. One of the milestones this app can optionally auto-detect from the temperature curve instead of requiring a manual click.
DRY% (Dry percent)
What percentage of the whole roast, so far, was spent in the drying phase — the stretch from Charge to Dry End.
Drum
One of the three things this app can control on a real roaster — how fast the drum rotates, which tumbles the beans so they roast evenly. Not to be confused with DT, a temperature reading, not a control.
DT (Drum Temperature)
A separate temperature probe some roasters (the FZ-94 included) have, reading the air inside the drum chamber itself — not the beans (that's BT) and not a duplicate of the exhaust reading (that's ET). Not every unit has this probe wired up; "no data" for DT is often just how a particular machine is configured, not a fault.

E

ET (Environmental/Exhaust Temperature)
How hot the air immediately around the beans is, as opposed to the beans themselves (that's BT). ET reacts faster to a burner change than BT does, so a sudden move in ET is often the first hint of what BT is about to do a little later.

F

FC END (First Crack End)
The moment the audible cracking that marked First Crack Start tapers off and stops.
FC START (First Crack Start)
The moment the beans start audibly cracking — genuinely audible, a bit like popcorn, a real physical event rather than a guess. Typically happens somewhere around 196°C bean temperature, though the exact number varies by bean. One of the milestones this app can optionally auto-detect from the curve.
Feedback register Modbus
A register some devices offer purely for reading back what they're actually doing right now — e.g. a fan's real current speed — kept separate from whatever register you write a command to. Lets this app confirm a command genuinely took effect, instead of just hoping the write worked.
Frequency register Modbus / FZ-94
On a VFD-driven motor (Air and Drum on the FZ-94), this is the register that actually sets the speed, once a separate run/stop register has told the motor to run at all.

H

heater_pct
The internal name this app's API and Device Profiles use for the Burner control — same thing the app's own screens label "Burner," just written differently in code/JSON than what you see in the UI.

K

K-type (thermocouple)
A common, inexpensive type of temperature probe, named for the pair of metals inside it. The Mastech MS6514 meter this app can read from directly uses two K-type probes, wired to BT and ET.

M

Maillard% named for the Maillard reaction, the browning chemistry
What percentage of the whole roast, so far, was spent in the Maillard/browning phase — the stretch from Dry End to First Crack Start.

P

Parity Modbus / FZ-94
An extra error-checking bit added to each byte sent over the serial connection — a simple way for the receiving end to catch a corrupted byte. The FZ-94 uses no parity at all ("N").

R

Register / Register address Modbus
A single labeled storage slot inside a device's own controller, holding one value — think of it like one labeled cubbyhole in a wall of them. "Register 5" on the FZ-94's burner controller is specifically where its temperature setpoint lives; reading or writing that register is how this app checks or changes it.
RoR (BT) / ΔBT (Rate of Rise)
How fast the bean temperature is climbing (or falling) right now, in degrees per minute — not the temperature itself, but its speed of change. Roasters watch this closely: a sudden crash usually means too little heat for where the roast currently is, a spike can mean scorching risk.
RoR (ET)
The same idea as RoR (BT) above, but for the exhaust temperature (ET) instead of the bean temperature.

S

SC END (Second Crack End)
The moment the (quieter, higher-pitched) Second Crack sound stops. Reaching Second Crack at all is a choice, not a default — most lighter roasts are dropped well before it.
SC START (Second Crack Start)
A second, distinct round of audible cracking that can happen later in a darker roast, driven by a different underlying process than First Crack.
Slave ID Modbus / FZ-94
Several separate devices (the bean-temperature probe, the burner controller, the fan motor...) can all share one physical wire back to this app. Slave ID is simply which one you're addressing on that shared wire — like a phone extension number at an office where everyone shares one main line in. On the FZ-94, BT is slave 11, Air is slave 1, Drum is slave 2, and so on — every probe/motor has its own number even though they're all on the same cable.
Stop bits Modbus / FZ-94
Another piece of the serial "both ends must agree" framing (along with Baud rate, Bytesize, and Parity) — marks where one byte ends and the next begins. The FZ-94 uses 2.
SV (Setpoint Value)
The target a device's own built-in controller is actively trying to hold, in its native unit. The FZ-94's Burner is the clearest example: it doesn't take a power percentage at all — you give it a target drum temperature (its SV, in °C), and the machine's own thermostat-like logic switches its heating elements on and off to hold that target. This app shows Burner both ways — a familiar 0–100% slider, and a second slider for the SV directly in °C — moving either one moves the other, since they're just two units for the same underlying target.

T

Temp channel Device Profile
One of the temperature probes a Device Profile knows how to read — BT, ET, DT, or an extra one you name yourself (e.g. a flue probe on a roaster that has one).
TURNING POINT
Right after cold green beans go into a hot roaster, the temperature briefly drops before it starts climbing — the cold beans are soaking up heat faster than the roaster can add it back in. Turning Point is the moment that reverses: the lowest point on the curve, where BT stops falling and starts rising for good. It's a pure observation (the bottom of a dip), not a judgment call, which is why this app always detects it automatically rather than asking for a manual click.

V

VFD (Variable Frequency Drive) Modbus / FZ-94
The type of motor controller the FZ-94 uses to run Air and Drum — it adjusts the electrical frequency going to the motor to change its speed. Needs two separate writes to actually move: a run/stop command first, then a separate speed command, which is why Air and Drum are slightly more involved to control than the simpler Direct register mechanism.

W

Write register Modbus
The register a Direct register control actually writes its value to — simpler roasters that don't use a VFD or a setpoint-style burner take a plain number straight to one register like this.
