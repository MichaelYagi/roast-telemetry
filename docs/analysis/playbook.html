Roast Analysis Playbook

A worked example for the Analysis page's three tabs, ordered here by how often you'd actually check each one — not the left-to-right order they appear in on the page. One running example throughout: a shop roasting Ethiopia Yirgacheffe every week. The numbers below are illustrative, not real data — swap in your own bean and figures.

1. Drift — catches the one-off

Flags any roast more than two standard deviations from how that same bean usually roasts, across every number that matters for repeatability — not just whatever happens to be plotted on Explore. Always grouped by beans, no setup needed. Check it after every roast, or in a weekly batch review.

Example: Underdeveloped Batch · Ethiopia Yirgacheffe · Development time: 0:10 (−2.0 SD) — usual for this bean: 1:43 ± 0:41.

What it means: one batch, not a trend — something was different about this roast specifically, worth a second look before it ships.

2. Trends — catches the slow slide

A fixed small chart per key number (roast duration, drop temperature, development time, development %, weight loss), each plotted against date with a rolling average, regardless of bean. Check it on a schedule — weekly or monthly — independent of any one bean.

Example: Drop temperature, rolling average, last 6 weeks: 178.4°C → 181.6°C (+3.2°C).

What it means: a gradual shift, not one bad batch — probe calibration, burner efficiency and airflow are the usual suspects.

3. Explore — finds the relationship

Pick any two numbers (or Date) for Across/Up, and a group to colour by. Each roast is a point — for finding what correlates with what, or comparing groups against each other. Reach for it when something needs explaining, or a recipe needs tuning.

Example: Across = Charge temperature, Up = Development time, Colour by = Beans — the Ethiopia Yirgacheffe dots slope downward: higher charge temperature, shorter development time, visible across 18 points.

What it means: a lever you didn't know you had — useful for dialing in a recipe, or explaining why one roast tasted different from the last.

Action plan

Same order as above: chase the specific signal first, the slow one second, the investigative one when you actually need to change something.

  1. From Drift: open the flagged roast and check its tasting notes or outcome. If it tasted off, log why (bigger batch that day, a newly opened bag, something you got pulled away from) so it's not a mystery next time. If it tasted fine, your "normal" window might be tighter than it needs to be.
  2. From Trends: treat a multi-week creep as an equipment question, not a recipe one — check and clean the roaster, verify probe calibration if you can, then keep watching Trends over the next couple of weeks to confirm it actually stabilizes rather than keeps climbing.
  3. From Explore: pick a charge-temperature target that gives the development time you actually want, update the recipe or preset, then watch Trends and Drift over the next several batches to confirm the change stuck and didn't just move the problem somewhere else.

Drift and Explore's own Roasts that stand out list use the same threshold (two or more standard deviations, five or more roasts in the group). Drift always groups by beans; Roasts that stand out follows whatever Explore is currently grouped and plotted by.
